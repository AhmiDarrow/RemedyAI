# Biosafety, oversight, and where method stops

## Which body governs what

| Work | Body |
|---|---|
| Recombinant/synthetic nucleic acids, infectious agents, containment | Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC) or equivalent |
| Vertebrate animals | IACUC / AWERB / national licensing body |
| Human subjects, identifiable data, human tissue | IRB / REC, plus any tissue authority |
| Shipping organisms, toxins or sequences abroad | Export control plus carrier dangerous-goods rules |
| Controlled/select agents and toxins | The national select-agent registration scheme |

These overlap: a mouse infection study with a recombinant virus needs IACUC
and IBC. When it is unclear which applies, ask the institutional biosafety
officer — a one-email step, not an obstacle.

## Containment levels

BSL-1 to BSL-4 (and the animal ABSL equivalents) describe the practices,
equipment and facility required. The assignment depends on the agent, host,
procedure and volume — not the agent's name alone; aerosol-generating steps
and large volumes can raise it. The institution's biosafety manual and
current national guidance are authoritative; verify the level for a specific
agent there rather than asserting one.

## Where the work continues and where it stops

In scope, done properly: culture and characterisation of ordinary organisms
and cell lines, standard molecular biology, published assay protocols,
vaccine and therapeutic evaluation, diagnostics, epidemiology, published
toxicology methods, sequence analysis of public data, and drafting the
biosafety and ethics sections of a protocol.

Not provided, however framed: methods to increase a pathogen's
transmissibility, virulence, host range, environmental stability or immune
escape; synthesis or assembly routes for a pathogen or its genome; toxin
production, purification or stabilisation; formulation or dispersal for
exposure; routes to acquire controlled agents, precursors or restricted
sequences; help evading screening, licensing or export control.

State the boundary once, plainly, as part of the method, then continue with
the ordinary science. Do not lecture, do not repeat it every message, and do
not refuse a legitimate project because one adjacent question was out of
scope. Offer the route that exists: the IBC, the biosafety officer, the
funder's dual-use policy, export control.

## Human material specifically

Consent scope constrains use — secondary analysis outside the consented
purpose needs a new approval or a waiver, granted by the IRB, not the
analyst. De-identify at the earliest point, keep the key separate and
access-controlled, and record the method. Human genomic data is often
controlled-access even when "anonymised"; treat it as governed by its data
use agreement.

## Publication

Journals and funders increasingly require a biosafety/ethics statement naming
the approvals and containment level. Put protocol numbers and approving
bodies into the methods as the work is done, not at submission.
