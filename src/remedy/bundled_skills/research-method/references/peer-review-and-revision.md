# Peer review and revision

## Reading a review

Sort every comment into: factual error, missing evidence, unclear writing,
disagreement about interpretation, scope creep, or a request that cannot be
met. Reviewers are often right about the symptom and wrong about the cure -
address the symptom.

Count the work before promising it. New experiments, new cohorts, shared
instrument time or money go to the owner as a decision, not as a plan you
already committed to.

## The response letter

- Reproduce every comment verbatim, numbered, then respond under it.
- State what changed and where: "Revised, Methods p.7, lines 210-218", and
  quote the new text.
- Where you disagree, say so plainly with evidence, and offer the alternative
  you did make. Politeness is not agreement.
- Where you cannot do it, say why and what the manuscript now says instead
  (usually an added limitation).
- Never claim a change you did not make. Re-read the diff against the letter
  before sending.

## Revising without overclaiming

Pressure to strengthen conclusions is the most common way a paper becomes
wrong between submission and publication. New analyses requested by reviewers
are post-hoc: label them as such in the text, even when they support you.
Additional data changes the multiplicity picture - recount the family.

Keep a changelog of the revision alongside the deviations table.

## Reviewing someone else's work

Judge the question, the design, the analysis and whether the conclusions
follow. In that order.

- Read the methods before the results; check the abstract's claim against the
  results table last.
- Check n at every stage, the prespecification (registry entry, if any),
  effect sizes and intervals, and whether the multiplicity was handled.
- Separate "this is wrong" from "I would have done it differently".
- Be specific and actionable; cite what you assert, and only what you have
  read.
- Confidentiality: the manuscript is not yours to share, quote, or use. Do
  not paste it into anything that retains it. Declare conflicts and decline
  when you have one.
- The owner decides whether a review is accepted, signed, or declined.

## Preprints and versions

If a preprint exists, update it when the paper changes materially, and link
the published version. A cited preprint that has since been published gets
its citation updated - `cite_check` will flag the mismatch, and the published
version is the one that goes in the bib.

## After acceptance

Check the proofs against the accepted manuscript number by number, especially
tables, units and author affiliations. Deposit the data and code archive,
register the DOI in `CITATION.cff`, and record the ledger run ids that
produced the final figures.
