# Qualitative methods

Qualitative work answers different questions — how something is experienced, by
what mechanism, in whose terms — and is judged by different standards. It is not
decoration on a quantitative paper, and it is not "small-n quantitative".

## Design choices to make explicit

- **Approach**: thematic analysis, grounded theory, framework analysis,
  interpretative phenomenological analysis, ethnography, discourse analysis.
  They have different aims; naming one and doing another is the common flaw.
- **Sampling logic**: purposive, maximum-variation, theoretical, snowball. Say
  the logic and the criteria, not just the count.
- **Stopping rule**: information power or saturation, with the evidence — which
  codes stopped appearing, at which interview. "Saturation was reached" without
  detail is an assertion.
- **Data generation**: semi-structured interview, focus group, participant
  observation, documents. Ship the topic guide. Note who interviewed, where,
  how long, in which language, and whether transcribed verbatim.

## Analysis you can audit

- Keep an audit trail: raw transcripts, coded extracts, codebook versions,
  analytic memos, and the dated decisions that changed the framework.
- Codebook: code name, definition, inclusion and exclusion criteria, an example
  and a near-miss. Version it in the repo.
- Multiple coders are required when the claim is a **count** ("most
  participants said"); report an agreement statistic and the adjudication
  procedure. When the claim is interpretive, multiple readers are for
  challenging the reading, not for a kappa — say which mode you are in.
- Report disconfirming cases. A theme that every extract supports usually means
  the search was one-sided.
- Reflexivity: state the researcher's position relative to the participants and
  how it plausibly shaped access and answers. This is method, not confession.

## Reporting

Quote enough to let the reader judge the interpretation, with a participant
identifier that cannot re-identify. Say how many participants each theme drew
on. Distinguish participants' words from your analytic language. COREQ and SRQR
are the usual reporting checklists — confirm the current version from the
publisher or the EQUATOR Network rather than reciting items.

## Mixed methods

State the design: sequential (qual then quant, or quant then qual) or
concurrent, and what integration means here — sampling, instrument development,
explanation of an anomaly, or triangulation. Write the integration in the
results, not just the discussion. Divergence between strands is a finding worth
reporting, not an error to reconcile away.

## Ethics

Consent covers quotation and archiving, which most participants do not think
about at the time — ask specifically. De-identify at transcription:
pseudonymise, remove employers, places, rare roles and dates. See
`human-subjects-ethics.md`.
