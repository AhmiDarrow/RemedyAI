# Human participants: ethics, consent and data protection

These are procedure steps that happen before collection, not paperwork added at
submission. Remedy does the ordinary work — protocol text, consent language,
de-identification plans, data-management plans — and points at the governing
review rather than around it. A study design that avoids review, re-identifies
people, or collects data participants did not agree to is not something to help
optimise; the answer is to route it back through the ethics committee.

## Review

- Ethics/IRB approval before recruitment, with the protocol number in the
  paper. Amendments need approval too.
- Exempt or minimal-risk categories exist in many jurisdictions, but the
  committee decides that, not the researcher.
- Secondary analysis of existing data still usually needs a determination, and
  the original consent must cover the new use. Check the data use agreement.
- Vulnerable participants (children, prisoners, patients, the researcher's own
  employees, people who cannot consent) trigger extra safeguards and usually
  assent plus guardian consent.

## Consent

Plain language: purpose, what participation involves, duration, risks and
benefits, compensation, voluntariness and the right to withdraw (and by when
withdrawal is still possible), who sees the data, how long it is kept, whether
it is archived or shared, and contacts for the researcher and the committee.
Separate consent for optional elements: recording, quotation, archiving,
recontact.

## Deception and debriefing

Deception requires a justification that no non-deceptive design answers the
question, committee approval, and a debrief that explains the deception, the
reason, and offers withdrawal of the data afterwards. Debrief text is written
and approved before the study runs, not improvised.

## Data protection

- Collect the minimum. If the analysis does not need birth dates, postcodes, IP
  addresses or open-text names, do not store them.
- Separate the identifier key from the analysis data, store it separately with
  restricted access, and delete it when the study allows.
- Pseudonymised is not anonymised. Quasi-identifiers (age, sex, region,
  occupation, rare condition, exact dates) re-identify people in combination —
  coarsen or suppress small cells before sharing.
- Free text and qualitative transcripts leak identity; de-identify at
  transcription and re-scan before release.
- Know which regime applies (for example GDPR in the EU/UK, HIPAA for US health
  data, national law elsewhere) and record the lawful basis, retention period
  and transfers in the data-management plan. Verify current requirements with
  the institution's data-protection officer, not from memory.

## In the manuscript

An ethics statement naming the committee and approval reference, the consent
procedure, the de-identification applied, and where the data can and cannot be
shared and why. "Data available on request" needs a named custodian and a real
process.
